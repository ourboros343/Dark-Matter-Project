Dark Matter

The Invisible Matter That Shapes Our Universe

What Is Dark Matter?

Dark matter is a mysterious form of matter that cannot be seen because it does not produce, reflect, or absorb light. Scientists know it exists because of the way it affects gravity and the movement of stars and galaxies.

How Was It Discovered and By Whom?

In 1933, astronomer Fritz Zwicky observed that galaxies in the Coma Cluster were moving too quickly. Based on the amount of visible matter, they should have flown apart. He suggested that invisible matter was providing extra gravity.


In the 1970s, astronomer Vera Rubin studied how galaxies rotate. Her observations showed that stars farther from the center were moving much faster than expected, providing strong evidence for dark matter.

What Is It Responsible For?

Dark matter helps keep galaxies together. Its gravity prevents stars from drifting away and helps form large structures throughout the universe.

How Do We Know It Exists?

  • Stars move faster than visible matter can explain.
  • Galaxies remain together when they should fly apart.
  • Light bends around massive objects more than expected.
  • Computer models of the universe work best when dark matter is included.

Where Is Dark Matter Found?

Scientists believe dark matter exists throughout the universe. It surrounds galaxies in large invisible halos and is also found between galaxies.

Interesting Facts

  • Dark matter makes up about 85% of all matter in the universe.
  • Scientists have never directly seen dark matter.
  • Dark matter may help explain how galaxies formed.
  • It is one of the biggest mysteries in modern science.
